## `01-api-contract.md`

### 1. [HIGH] — ✅ FIXED

**Issue.** §3b (line 902) states flatly: 'validation is free and leaves no trace, rejection is permanent.' Every other chapter says the opposite — 02 §0.1.5 and §9.1 grade it CONFIRMED that each *failed ValidateBid* writes an entity-visible row; 04 M8, 05 §8/G-07, 07 principle 4 and 08 DL-03 build the ≤2-rejection-per-auction budget on that. Root SOT.md §4.10 reproduces the same self-contradiction inside one bullet ('a bid that fails validation is not discarded, it is recorded' immediately followed by 'Validation is free and leaves no trace'). An engineer implementing from 01 — which the set presents as the evidence base — would build no budget at all. The inference is also over-graded: the captured log rows carry subtype `Logs_Search_NewBid` ('Nuevo Lance'), and 09 shows a distinct `Logs_Search_SubmitBidding` ('Presentar lances'); nothing in evidence distinguishes 'validation logged a row' from 'the UI's submit ran server-side validation and logged a row'.

**Recommended fix.** Pick one reading and propagate it. Recommend correcting 01 §3b to 'a rejected validation is permanently logged; whether a successful validation is logged is unknown', and downgrading 02 finding 0.1.5 from CONFIRMED to DERIVED since the log subtype does not distinguish validate from submit. Add that distinction as an explicit open question resolvable from the same O2 capture.

### 14. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** Participant counts are inconsistent across every chapter and none matches the captured payload. 01 §2b says '48 bidders were registered'; 01 §4's `chat.onConnected` payload — the only roster snapshot in evidence — contains exactly 27 companyCodes; 00 §4 says '44 distinct companies seen in the roster' and '~20+ connected simultaneously'; 02 §8 says 'the full connected roster (44 companies observed)' citing that same message; 06 §1.3 and 07 §4.2 say '44 **rival** companies' (which would make 45 total, since 700961121 appears in the roster). The churn statistics in 00 §3.2 (100 disconnects, 199 reconnects, 36 of 44 dropped, median 4, worst 19) appear in no other chapter and are traceable to no artefact in 01.

**Recommended fix.** Pick the derivation (roster union over all `onConnected`/`onNewUserConnected` frames, presumably), state it once with the tool that computed it, and make 01 the single source the others reference. Fix the rivals-vs-total ambiguity in 06 §1.3 and 07 §4.2, and record the churn statistics in 01 alongside the timing data so they are auditable.

### 31. [LOW] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** Line 852 of the preserved-headers block reads `User-Agent: Mozilla/5.0 (Windows NT 10.0; Win64; x64) AppleWebKit/537.36 (KHTML, like Gecko) Chrome/<IP_REDACTED> Safari/537.36`. The Chrome version has been replaced with the IP-redaction token, proving the redactor in `tools/digest_har.py` matches dotted numerals rather than IP addresses in context. 00 §5 and §5.1 present that tool as the control that makes the derived documents credential-free ('redacts credentials as it writes'). A redactor demonstrably matching the wrong things has not been precision-tested, and the same imprecision that over-matches here could under-match elsewhere. No actual credential leaked — this is about the control, not a live exposure.

**Recommended fix.** Tighten the IP pattern to require four dotted octets with word boundaries and no preceding `/`, add unit tests over the real header corpus asserting both no-false-positive (Chrome versions, `AppleWebKit/537.36`, decree numbers like `2.2.1.2.1.2.6`) and no-false-negative (JWTs, `token=`, `Set-Cookie`, IPv4/IPv6), and regenerate 01.

## `02-auction-mechanics.md`

### 2. [HIGH] — ✅ FIXED

**Issue.** §0.1 finding 9 states: 'Cross-auction counterexample (UNP SASI-064-2026): a rejected validation in a *different* auction reported a `minBid` equal to 2% of the last valid lance... CONFIRMED as an observation of that auction.' The evidence says no such thing. 06 §1.4 sources the same fact to the *pliego text* ('a live 2026 pliego (UNP SASI-064-2026) instead specifies "2% respecto del último lance válido"'), and 06 §3.4's own live query for that tender returns `estado: Publicado`, `fase: Clarification submission`, `fecha_de_recepcion_de: 2026-08-12` — offers are not yet due, so no auction has run and no ValidateBid rejection can exist. The mis-attribution propagates as fact into 04 M3, 07 principle 4, 08 OQ-03 and DL-08, and this single counterexample is the entire justification for the project's δ posture. 02 §0 defines CONFIRMED as 'directly present in a captured HTTP payload'; this meets none of it.

**Recommended fix.** Rewrite finding 0.1.9 to state its true provenance (a published pliego clause for a tender whose auction has not occurred), grade it CONFIRMED-as-pliego-clause / DERIVED-as-platform-behaviour, and correct 04 M3, 07 §1.4 and 08 OQ-03. The conclusion (never assume the base) survives; the evidence grade does not.

### 4. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** Rounding direction conflates the *step* with the *bid*, and the conflation is coded. §4.5 correctly says rounding `step_i` up makes the constraint stricter, then adds 'Apply `round_up` to **any bid we emit**', and RULE M-5 says `bid = ceil(bid_raw_pesos)`. But the emitted bid is `T = ref − step`, already at the maximum legal value; ceiling it pushes it *above* the legal threshold and it is rejected. 04 §6.1 codes exactly this: `t = round_up_to_grid(t, view.ruleset)` applied to `min(own_ceiling, best − tick)` with ROUND_CEILING. The doc's worked example only ever rounds the *step* up (1,156,705.23 → 1,156,706), which is safe, then generalises the wrong lesson.

**Recommended fix.** Split the rule explicitly: `step` rounds UP (stricter, safe for legality); the emitted `bid` rounds DOWN to the peso grid and is then re-checked against floor F, with STOP if the floored value breaches F. Update §4.5, RULE M-5 and 04 §6.1's `round_up_to_grid` call, and add a property test asserting every emitted total satisfies `T ≤ ref − step` *after* grid quantisation.

### 18. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §4.3 grades `minimumBidType == 0` as DERIVED ('probably "Value"/"Amount"', absolute amount per item), and §4.5 then computes `step_i = d_i if minimumBidType == 0` — authorising a real bid on an enum meaning that has never been observed. RULE M-1 fails closed only for values outside {0,1}, so the one unverified interpretation inside the allowed set is the one that gets to price a lance. This violates §0's own contract ('never let an ASSUMED claim alone authorize a bid') in the arithmetic that determines how much margin is conceded per step.

**Recommended fix.** Restrict M-1's allowed set to {1} (the only CONFIRMED value) until a type-0 auction is observed; route `minimumBidType == 0` to `DEGRADED_UNKNOWN_RULE` with human per-bid confirmation, exactly as for values 2+.

### 19. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** RULE M-2 (dual-candidate maximal strictness — 'a candidate bid is legal only if it satisfies every live interpretation... the engine applies the maximum candidate decrement') is implemented nowhere. 04's `next_legal_total` takes a single `view.min_step`, and 04 §3.4's `AuctionView` exposes a single scalar `min_step`. Separately, M-2's direction has an unacknowledged cost: with candidates {% of initial (large, fixed)} and {% of last lance (shrinking)}, the maximum decrement is always the initial-based one, so the rule systematically concedes more margin per step than required — directly against 04 §2.1's corollary 2 ('It never "spends" price to accelerate toward the floor') and accelerating the descent toward the floor.

**Recommended fix.** Add a `min_step_candidates: tuple[Centavos, ...]` field to `AuctionView` and implement M-2 in `next_legal_total`; state in 02 §4.6 that pre-calibration bidding costs margin, and prefer resolving the base from `GetAuction.minBidDifference` plus the first *accepted* bid before placing any large-step lance.

### 21. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** RULE C-2 is internally inconsistent and is violated by the chapter that implements it. C-2 mandates '≥ 1 sample / 10 s while `Started`; ≥ 1 sample / 2 s during the final 120 s', then in the next sentence says 'Never below ~2 s: that becomes abusive polling' — mandating the exact rate it calls abusive. 03 §3.3 implements 15 s for ARMED (violating the 10 s floor) and 5 s for ENDGAME (violating the 2 s requirement), and ENDGAME is defined as ≤60 s to endDate, so the 'final 120 s' window is never covered at the mandated rate at all. 03 acknowledges only that 'ours is deliberately gentler', without noting it contradicts a normative RULE.

**Recommended fix.** Rewrite C-2 as a range with an explicit abuse floor (e.g. '1 sample per 10–15 s while Started; 1 per 5 s in the final 120 s; never below 2 s'), align 03 §3.3 to it, and extend ENDGAME entry to 120 s if the tighter clock cadence is genuinely needed there.

### 23. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §7.2 claims 'Extensions are unbounded in count — no maximum appears anywhere in the payloads, templates, or locale bundle.' The locale bundle contradicts this: `Settings_Maximum_Bids_Extra_Time` ('Contador de máximo de lances en tiempo extra') and `Settings_Maximum_Bids_Extra_Time_AltText` are present in both 01 (line 73) and 09 (lines 320–321), and `GetAuctionSimple` returns `extraTimeMaximumBids: null` / `startingExtraTimeMaximumBids: null` — the cap is a real platform feature that merely happened to be unset here. 04 M5 hardens this into a mechanism fact ('`extraTimeMaximumBids: null` → **unlimited extensions**', graded OBSERVED) and 07 principle 2 generalises to 'unlimited times'. Against hard constraint 1, the engine will meet auctions with a finite extension budget and no chapter specifies the behaviour.

**Recommended fix.** Correct §7.2's 'appears nowhere' claim, scope 'unlimited' to `extraTimeMaximumBids == null`, and specify the finite-budget case: track the counter and note that the last extension is the real deadline, which materially changes the endgame policy.

### 32. [LOW] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §7.1 grades the unit of `extraTimePeriod: 3` as 'CONFIRMED (unit corroborated by the log message rendering seconds: 3 min = "180 segundos")'. The corroboration is circular: `Log_Message_AuctionExtended` = 'Subasta Prorrogada por {0} segundos' proves the *log* renders seconds, not that the config field is minutes — and no extension fired in the capture, so no `{0}` value was ever observed. The genuine corroboration exists and is uncited: 09 lists `Settings_Extra_Time_Interval` = 'Tiempo extra minutos'. A CONFIRMED grade resting on an invalid inference undermines the grading system the chapter depends on.

**Recommended fix.** Replace the cited corroboration with `Settings_Extra_Time_Interval`. While there, note a second locale mismatch the chapter relies on: `Settings_Allow_Pause` renders as 'Permitir arranque manual' ('allow manual start'), not 'allow pause' — §2.2's reading of `pauseAllow` should acknowledge the label does not support it.

## `04-strategy-engine.md`

### 3. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §6.1 `next_legal_total` computes `t = min(own_last − δ, best_serious_visible − tick)` — one *centavo* below the best bid. This contradicts (a) 02 RULE R-2, which requires `T ≤ ref − step_total` with `ref = min(own_last, best)`, i.e. a full δ below best, and (b) 05 §2.4's conservative recommendation `min(own_last_bid − margin, global_best_bid − margin)`. Worse, the baseline logic (`baseline = my_last_bid; own_ceiling = baseline − delta`) is exactly candidate rules R1/R2, which 02 §5.2's four-observation table explicitly *refutes* (R1 ✗, R2 ✗) in favour of R4. The engine chapter implements a rule the mechanics chapter already ruled out, and every bid it produces against a visible best would be a `BidErrMin` rejection — burning the two-rejection budget at the decisive moment.

**Recommended fix.** Rewrite `next_legal_total` to implement 02 RULE R-1/R-2 verbatim: `ref = min(own_last_server, current_best)`, `t ≤ ref − step_total`, with the R4 fallback regime (`ref − step ≤ 0`) mapping to STOP. Use the *unfiltered* server best for the legality threshold and the probe-filtered best only for the rank/HOLD decision, and say so explicitly.

### 6. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** The floor story's headline claim is false as written. §4.2 and 07 §6 both state the layers are independent and 'each would stop a below-floor lance even if all the others were deleted.' L0 is a signed-config/provenance control — at runtime it stops nothing. L4 is post-submit *detection*, and both chapters state elsewhere that a lance cannot be retracted, so L4 by construction cannot stop a below-floor lance either. That leaves three preventive layers, two of which (L1 BidGuard, L2 FloorClampedTotal) live in the same `guard/` package with the same author, review and version pin. 05 G-03 tacitly concedes it by enumerating 'guard package (L1), type-level clamp (L2), gateway byte-level re-check (L3), plus floor-sheet provenance and latched auto-kill' — three enforcement points dressed as five.

**Recommended fix.** Restate honestly: three independent *preventive* layers (L1, L2, L3), with L0 as provenance and L4 as detection-and-containment. Either accept that framing or make L1/L2 genuinely independent (different package, different reviewer, independently derived floor value). Keep the 'five layers' label only if each is characterised by what it can and cannot stop.

### 12. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** The `fire_at = end_at − 45 s` doctrine rests on an assumption three other chapters forbid or contradict. 04 M5 asserts flatly '`activateExtraTimeWinner: true` → **even the leader's own lance extends**' and §7.1's diagram repeats it; 04 §7.3 and 07 §5.6 justify the 45 s figure as 'inside the closing window W = 60 s, so the lance both scores **and extends**'. But 02 RULE X-3 mandates the opposite for exactly this purpose ('never count on an extension we did not verifiably cause... the engine simply never plans a cycle that needs the extension to land'), 02 §7.4's worked timeline deliberately fires at T−90 s and T−80 s *outside* the window, 00 §7.3's KPI says the design intent is 'never inside the extension-trigger minute unless deliberate' (T−45 s is by definition inside a 60 s trigger minute), and 09's own platform string `Settings_Activate_Extra_Time_Winner` = 'Tiempo extra solo para lance ganador' is direct evidence for the restrictive reading that 04 dismisses without citing it.

**Recommended fix.** Cite `Settings_Activate_Extra_Time_Winner` in 02 §7.3 and 04 M5, downgrade M5's leader-extends assertion to ASSUMED-and-probably-false, and re-derive `fire_at` under X-3's pessimistic assumption. Then reconcile 00 §7.3's KPI wording with whatever number survives — currently the KPI and the parameter are mutually exclusive.

### 13. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §6.1's `next_legal_total` reads `view.my_initial_proposal` and §6.5's `Bracket.decide` reads `view.start_reference` — neither field exists in the `AuctionView` contract declared in 04 §3.4 or 07 §4.4. The first-lance path is therefore undefined in the specification, and 04 O-SE5 concedes we do not know where the initial proposal value is exposed by the API. Compounding this, 03 §7's 'first-bid caveat' and 09 §2 (`BidWarn_Min_Bid_Diff_Desc`: 'Si es su primer lance, este tiene que ser igual al precio base') suggest the first lance may be required to equal the base price — which would make `own_ceiling = my_initial_proposal − δ` wrong for the very first bid. Yet 08 Phase 4 ('First armed live bid') lists no dependency on resolving this.

**Recommended fix.** Add `my_initial_proposal` and `start_reference` to the `AuctionView` contract in both 04 §3.4 and 07 §4.4 with their provenance, or remove them from the code sketches. Make O-SE5 and the first-bid-equals-base-price question blocking exit criteria for Phase 4, since the first armed lance is by definition a first lance.

### 29. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** The serious-bid filter creates an unhandled losing scenario that is precisely the captured auction. With a rival probe standing at 0,00 COP, the filter yields `my_rank_serious == 1`, so every strategy returns HOLD (§6.2/§6.3/§6.4 all open with `if view.my_rank == 1: return Decision(HOLD)`). §5 concedes 'the award decision at close may still treat a probe as standing' — so the specified behaviour is to hold and lose to a 0-COP bid. 02 §5.3 shows the same probe also mechanically locks the ladder under R4, meaning no counter-bid is even legal. No chapter specifies what the operator should do, what the panel should show, or whether this is an alarm condition rather than a silent HOLD.

**Recommended fix.** Add an explicit `PROBE_LOCKED` state: when `my_rank_serious == 1` but `my_rank_actual > 1`, raise a P0 alarm rather than a silent HOLD, display both ranks prominently, and document the human options (accept the loss; challenge the artificially low offer post-award under D.1082 art. 2.2.1.1.2.2.4, which 05 §5 already establishes). This is a commercially decisive case, not an edge case.

### 30. [LOW] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** Determinism, which §3.4 calls 'load-bearing' and §9.5 property 7 tests ('identical event sequence ⇒ identical decision log'), is broken by the mandatory outer strategy. §6.5's `Bracket.decide` reads `self.steps_used` — per-instance mutable state not derived from the `AuctionView` and not reconstructible from it after the process restart 07 §5.7 mandates. Two runs over the same event sequence with different `steps_used` histories produce different decisions. Related defects in the same section: `MinStepResponder`, `PatientEndgame` and `Adaptive` all test `view.my_rank == 1` without handling `my_rank is None`, which VIS-2 (02 §8) says must degrade to HOLD but which here falls through to the bidding path.

**Recommended fix.** Move `steps_used` and cumulative decrement into the `AuctionView` (both are reconstructible from `GetSupplierBids` after reconciliation, which is where they belong), and make every `my_rank` test explicitly handle `None` → HOLD. Add a property test that restarts the strategy mid-sequence and asserts an identical decision log.

## `05-compliance.md`

### 7. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** The five floor layers enforce the *signed sheet*, not the *statute*, and only 08 OQ-07 and 06 D2 admit it. 05 §1 Risk C, §5 and G-03 all claim the controls ensure the bot 'never bids below the lawful floor'; 08 OQ-07 says plainly 'a wrong FloorSheet defeats all five guard layers from above'; 06 §5.4 marks the pliego-staffing → 24h/30-day-post mapping — 'the single largest source of floor-price error' — as UNVERIFIED pending client input. 00 §7.1's invariant is correctly scoped ('below the approved floor sheet'); 04 §4 and 07 §6, where the claim is actually made, are not. A reader of the compliance chapter concludes the statutory floor is mechanically guaranteed; it is guaranteed only downstream of a human spreadsheet that no layer checks.

**Recommended fix.** State explicitly in 04 §4, 07 §6 and 05 G-03 that the layers guarantee floor-sheet conformance and that statutory conformance rests entirely on the L0 human gate. Escalate OQ-07/D2 to a blocking precondition for `armed` mode, and add an independent second computation of the floor (different person, different method) as the missing control.

### 11. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §2.4 claims 'The regulation confirms the *basis* of the calculation' — that D.1082 art. 2.2.1.1.1.3.1 permits Margen Mínimo 'en dinero o en un porcentaje del precio de inicio de la subasta', so the observed 1%-of-start behaviour 'is no longer an unexplained coincidence' and `minimumBidType: 1` 'almost certainly selects it'. This is an unsupported leap and it collides with 06 §1.4, which documents a live 2026 pliego (UNP SASI-064-2026) specifying '2% respecto del último lance válido' — a base the decree definition quoted two paragraphs earlier in 05 §2.3 does *not* list. Either the enumeration is exhaustive (making the UNP configuration unlawful — a finding of real commercial value that nobody draws) or it is not (collapsing §2.4's 'confirms' claim). Neither chapter notices, and §2.4's confident framing pushes back against the project's own hard-won 'never assume the base' posture.

**Recommended fix.** Downgrade §2.4 from 'the regulation confirms' to 'the regulation permits one of the two observed bases'; add the observation that the UNP pliego's stated base is not among the decree's enumerated options and flag it for counsel as a possible challenge ground; cross-link 06 §1.4 so the two chapters read as one analysis.

### 24. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** G-06 claims to enforce ToS §IV.1.j ('one user session at a time') via 'One runner per (auctionId, companyCode) via exclusive lockfile'. That lockfile does not implement that duty: it is keyed on the auction, so N concurrent runners for N different auctions on the same credential are explicitly permitted (07 §4.3 and §11 confirm one process per auction). Two STARCOOP auctions the same afternoon means two concurrent sessions on one `personal e intransferible` user. OQ-08 ('Are redundant bidders safe?') is open and marked 'mitigated by design' on the strength of this same lockfile.

**Recommended fix.** Key the exclusive lock on `companyCode` alone, or add a second account-level lock; or state explicitly that concurrent auctions are out of scope until OQ-08 resolves. Update G-06's control column so the stated mechanism actually implements the stated duty.

### 25. [MEDIUM] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §0 point 3 states as a headline epistemic guarantee: 'Nothing here is a citation that was not actually opened.' §13 then lists, under 'Referenced from general legal knowledge / secondary confirmation — counsel to confirm exact current text', nine instruments cited throughout the chapter: Ley 80, Ley 1150 arts. 2/3/5, Ley 527 art. 7 (the basis of the entire electronic-signature analysis and Risk B), Ley 1474 art. 27 / CP art. 410A (the criminal red line, §4.2), Ley 1340, Ley 1581, D.1070/D.1561 (Risk C, the floor's legal source) and the Supervigilancia resolution. The chapter's most load-bearing legal claims sit in the unfetched set while its introduction asserts the opposite.

**Recommended fix.** Rewrite §0 point 3 to 'every source is labelled with how it was obtained; several central instruments were not fetched and are flagged in §13', and add inline UNVERIFIED markers at the point of use for Ley 527 art. 7 (§1 Risk B, §3.4, §7) and CP art. 410A (§4.2), not only in the appendix.

### 10. [HIGH] — 📋 OPEN

**Issue.** §5.2's tariff table — the Layer-0 input to a floor the project calls statutory — applies 'SMMLV 2026 = $1,750,905' to rows dated 2025-07-01, 2025-07-15, 2025-12-25 and 2027-07-01. The SMMLV figure and its source (Decreto 1469 de 2025) carry no UNVERIFIED marker despite §7's blanket verification standard, and a value of 1,750,905 implies a step change of roughly 23% over the 2025 SMMLV — large enough that it should not be asserted without a fetched primary source. ## Gaps: topics no chapter covers

- Multi-lot / multi-item runtime model. `AuctionView` (04 §3.4, 07 §4.4) is scalar — one `my_last_bid`, one `my_rank`, one `min_step`, one `floor` — while 02 §6.3 requires fully independent ref/step/rank/floor per lot for `AuctionByLot`/`AuctionByItem`. Hard constraint 1 (any number of lots, single- or multi-lot) is not met by any specified contract, and no chapter specifies the multi-lot decision loop, per-lot bracket envelope, or per-lot ValidateBid budget.

- Fail-closed rule for unsupported auction shapes. `Forward` and `testAuction` have explicit refusal rules (02 V-1/V-2), but there is none for `auctionVersionCode != AuctionbyProposal`, `auctionEvaluationTypeCode != BestPrice`, or non-null `scoreCalcFormula` / non-empty `evaluationRules` — even though 05 R-12 asserts the bot 'refuses to arm on unsupported types' and 05 §2.2 documents that multi-variable auctions exist in law (D.1082 art. 2.2.1.2.1.1.1).

- Which clock stamps the `Date` field of the bid body. The captured `ValidateBid` body carries `2026-03-02T20:30:40.104Z`, which is the operator's local clock (+13.15 s skew) and maps to server 20:30:26.95. No chapter says whether the bot writes server time or local time, despite an entire chapter on clock discipline; 02 §6.2's field-formula table omits `Date` entirely.

- Auction close and outcome handling. Nothing specifies how the system learns it won or lost, what it does with `showFinalRank` / `Sidebar_Won` / `auctionFinisheds`, how the final reconciliation proves our standing lance, or the handoff to award/contract. 00 §7.2's win-rate KPI cites 'award records' that no runtime component is specified to read.

- Validation of the floor *number* as opposed to the floor plumbing. Five layers protect against bidding below the sheet; nothing validates the sheet's arithmetic. 08 OQ-07 concedes 'a wrong FloorSheet defeats all five guard layers from above' and 06 D2 marks the `post_24h30d_equivalence` mapping UNVERIFIED, but no chapter specifies back-testing against past awards, an independent second costing computation, or a sanity band against `precio_base`.

- Data retention and deletion. 07 §11 says 'audit chains and floor sheets are retained per 05-compliance'; 05 never states a retention period, a deletion policy, or where sealed audit bundles live. 05 §6.3 requires the per-auction HMAC salt be destroyed at close but never says where it was stored.

- Operator continuity. 05 §10.2 requires a named operator 'available for the full auction window + extensions', and extensions are unbounded. No chapter covers operator incapacitation, shift handover mid-auction, or a 10-minute auction that runs 90 minutes past the operator's availability.

- Threat model for the bot's own control plane. Panel over WireGuard, Telegram `/kill` with a shared secret, kill-file, OS signal, and a noVNC-exposed live authenticated SECOP session — no chapter analyses compromise of any of these, nor specifies Telegram secret rotation (SC-064 says it is 'documented' but nothing documents it).

- Concurrent auctions on one account. One runner per auction is permitted by design, so two STARCOOP auctions the same day means two runners sharing one credential — unexamined against ToS §IV.1.j and OQ-08, and the `(auctionId, companyCode)` lockfile does not prevent it.

- Downside analysis of the vendor outreach proposed in 03 §8 Step 1 and 05 §3.7. Both recommend formally asking Vortal and CCE about supplier-side automation. Neither considers that a written query puts CCE on notice and, given the §VII.A.f preventive-suspension power the chapter itself documents, may create the exposure it is meant to close. No decision criteria and no fallback if the answer is 'no'.

- Effort, schedule and cost. 08 gives per-ticket sizes but no rolled-up schedule, no headcount, and no budget for the mandatory external inputs (Colombian procurement counsel, competition-law specialist, costing staff time per tender, VPS).

## Reviewer verdict

No credential leaks. I grepped all of docs/ (plus the referenced SOT.md and fixtures) for eyJ-prefixed JWTs, bearer tokens, token= values, cookie headers, ASP.NET session cookie names, base64 blobs ≥40 chars, IPv4 literals and password strings. Every hit was prose *about* the leaked bundle, a redaction placeholder (<IP_REDACTED>), a decree article number, or a SECOP URL path. The docs' own claim (00 §5.1 point 4) that "no real JWT, cookie, or password appears in any chapter" holds. The one defect in that area is a redactor precision bug (issue 32), not an exposure.

Everything else is less reassuring. This is an unusually well-written doc set — the evidence-grading system in 02 §0, the epistemic honesty in 05 §0/§13, the open-question registers — and that polish makes the failures harder to spot and more dangerous. Three patterns recur.

First, grade inflation on the load-bearing claims. The single counterexample justifying the whole δ posture (UNP SASI-064-2026) is graded CONFIRMED in 02 as an observed ValidateBid rejection, but 06's own live query shows that tender's offers are not due until 2026-08-12 — no auction, no rejection; the real source is a pliego clause. That mis-attribution propagates verbatim into 04, 07 and 08. Likewise 01 §3b says validation "leaves no trace" while five other chapters build a hard budget on the opposite; 02 §7.2 says no extension cap "appears anywhere in the locale bundle" when it plainly does; and a CONFIRMED unit grade rests on a circular inference.

Second, the normative chapters and the implementation chapters disagree on numbers that decide outcomes. abort_after is 8 s in 04/07 against a hard cutoff of 15–20 s derived in 02 from the same latency data — and 07's own arithmetic contradicts its own table two lines later. next_legal_total in 04 implements exactly the reference rules (R1/R2) that 02 §5.2 refutes, and undercuts by one centavo where 02 and 05 both require a full δ. Rounding conflates the step (up = safe) with the bid (up = illegal). Snapshot freshness is 10 s in the KPI and 5 s in the gate. RULE C-2 mandates a clock cadence it simultaneously calls abusive, and 03 violates it anyway.

Third, the floor-safety story is not airtight, and the docs know it in places they do not say it. "Five independent layers, each of which would stop a below-floor lance alone" is false: L0 is provenance, L4 is post-hoc detection of an irrevocable act, and L1/L2 share one package. What the layers actually guarantee is conformance to the *signed sheet* — and 08 OQ-07 concedes a wrong sheet "defeats all five guard layers from above", while 06 D2 marks the sheet's central input (post-equivalence mapping) UNVERIFIED and 06 §5.2's tariff table applies the 2026 SMMLV to 2025 and 2027 rows in contradiction of its own §5.3.3. The legal basis for that floor is cited three different ways in three chapters. So the deepest layer of the safety architecture is a human spreadsheet, computed from an unverified mapping and a possibly-wrong wage constant, over a statute nobody has agreed on — with no independent second computation anywhere in the design.

Finally, hard constraint 1 (universality) is not met by any specified contract: AuctionView is scalar in both 04 and 07, so AuctionByLot/AuctionByItem and multi-lot floors have no runtime model, and there is no fail-closed rule refusing to arm on them. Two code sketches in 04 reference AuctionView fields that do not exist, and the first-lance path depends on an unresolved open question that Phase 4 does not gate on.

Recommended order: fix the four evidence-integrity items (02 §0.1.9, 01 §3b, 02 §7.2, 02 §7.1) before anyone builds from these docs, since they corrupt the grading system everything else relies on; then reconcile the bid arithmetic (issues 3, 4, 5), where a wrong number costs money; then restate the floor claim honestly and add an independent check of the floor *number*; then close the multi-lot gap before claiming universality.
